Digital Catapult Process Simulator

A non-linear physics and Design of Experiments (DOE) simulation tool for process optimization and Six Sigma analysis.

🎯 Overview & Purpose

The Digital Catapult simulates the non-linear physics of a spring-loaded catapult, commonly used in Design of Experiments (DOE), Statistical Process Control (SPC), and Six Sigma training. Your goal is to understand factor interactions, optimize distance, and minimize shot variation.

⚙️ The 5 Input Factors (X's)

  • Pull-Back Angle (90° – 200°): Arm angle when loaded. 90° is straight up, 180° is horizontal, >180° pulls down. Controls primary elastic energy.
  • Stop Angle (90° – 120°): Angle where the arm hits the stop post, determining the initial launch trajectory vector.
  • Bungee Position (1.0 – 5.0): Rubber band tension setting (1 = loose/soft, 5 = stiff/maximum tension).
  • Arm Hole (1.0 – 5.0): Cup placement along the arm radius (1 = near pivot, 5 = tip). Affects rotational inertia and tangential release velocity.
  • Pin Elevation (1.0 – 5.0): Height adjustment on the stop pin, fine-tuning the effective release angle.

📊 The Responses (Y's)

  • Distance Thrown (m): Horizontal landing distance measured along the graduated ground ruler from the catapult pivot (0m).
  • Landing Variation (± m): Natural process standard deviation ($\sigma$). Higher tension and longer arm radius increase variability.
  • Release Velocity (m/s): Tangential velocity of the projectile upon cup release.

📋 How to Use & DOE Workflow

  1. Build Runs: Use Add Configuration to create trial runs, or directly edit existing rows.
  2. Excel Copy-Paste: Copy a 5-column DOE run table from Excel or Google Sheets (Ctrl+C) and paste (Ctrl+V) directly into the table.
  3. Simulate: Click Run All at Once to watch the animated catapult swing and ballistic trajectories. Each run is tagged with a colored landing flag.
  4. Analyze: Click Copy to Clipboard or Export CSV to import the response data into Excel, Minitab, or Python for regression and ANOVA.
